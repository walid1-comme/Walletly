# Walletly by Walid — positioning and launch copy

## The one sentence

Walletly reads every wallet you own, on every chain, on one page, without ever asking for a private key.

## The problem, stated the way a customer feels it

You have eleven wallets. One is cold. Three are for minting. A few are old burners you can't quite delete. To answer "what do I actually own," you open eleven explorer tabs, squint at eleven balances, and give up before you've added them together. When a project drops an allowlist, you paste addresses one at a time to see if you got in.

Nobody built the tool for this, because most portfolio trackers assume you're one person with one wallet. Serious holders are not.

## Who this is for

The person with five or more wallets who mints, farms, and holds across chains. Collab managers who run allowlists. Anyone who has ever lost track of which wallet holds what.

Not for: first-time buyers with a single wallet. They don't have the problem.

## Why people will pay $5

The value is not the data. Chain data is public and free. The value is the reading of it: every wallet in one pass, a matrix showing where value actually sits, allowlists checked in bulk, scam airdrops filtered out, and a history that tells you what changed.

Five dollars is less than the gas on one failed mint.

## The trust argument, which is the whole business

Every other crypto tool asks you to connect a wallet. That connection is the moment where things go wrong, and everyone in this space has watched someone get drained by a malicious approval.

Walletly cannot do that to you. It never asks for a private key, a seed phrase, a wallet connection, a signature or an approval. It reads public addresses, which are the same strings you already paste into Discord to receive an airdrop. There is no code in the app capable of moving an asset, and no permission it could use if there were.

Lead with this. It is a stronger hook than any feature.

## Objections and honest answers

**"Why would I pay for public data?"** You're paying for the reading, not the data. Same reason you pay for a spreadsheet when a calculator is free.

**"Is it safe?"** It only accepts public addresses. There's no connect button because there's no code that could use a connection.

**"Does it work with my chain?"** Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum, Optimism, Polygon, Ink and Robinhood Chain today. Ask and I'll add yours.

**"Can I try it first?"** Decide your answer and be consistent. Either offer a trial or don't, but never imply one that doesn't exist.
